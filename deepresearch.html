1. The Short Answer — read this first

Five things the record actually shows. Everything else in this document is detail.

  1. A police officer ran the biggest documented racket. Jagdish Bhola, a dismissed DSP and former international wrestler, was convicted twice — 24 years (2019, CBI court) and 10 years (2024, PMLA court) — in the racket the Enforcement Directorate valued at ₹6,000 crore. He named SAD leader Bikram Singh Majithia in his 2014 ED statement. Proven for the convictions; Charged for Majithia, trial ongoing.
  2. The money trail into politics is real but unfinished. Majithia was arrested in June 2025 for allegedly laundering ₹540 crore of "drug money" — including ₹161 crore in unexplained cash deposited in his family companies in 2007–09 and ₹141 crore routed through suspected foreign entities. A 40,000-page chargesheet was filed in August 2025. The High Court denied bail in December 2025, calling them "serious economic offences." Charged — not yet convicted.
  3. Protection works through five known mechanisms — police transfers and reinstatements, prison and parole failures, regulatory appointments, sealed-court reports that sit for years, and political accusation replacing accountability. Every mechanism has a documented example in this dossier. Mechanisms proven — the political direction behind most of them is not.
  4. On Arvind Kejriwal: no credible evidence on the public record that he personally profited from or directed the drug economy. What is documented: a ₹150–250 crore drug census whose report has not been released; a drug inspector arrested with ₹7+ crore in assets who is reported — not independently confirmed — to have been reinstated to a headquarters posting; documented prison drug rackets; and the killing of an anti-drug activist in 2026. The leap to "Kejriwal ran a commercial drug strategy" is, so far, a BJP leader's press conference, not evidence. Failures documented Personal-involvement claim: political assertion
  5. The single biggest accountability gap is a locked envelope. The 2018 Special Task Force report on the drug-police-politics nexus sat in a sealed cover at the Punjab & Haryana High Court for over three years. It is the source document for the Majithia case and for findings on senior police officers. Until it is fully unsealed, every party's claims rest on the same incomplete record. Sealed status proven

4. How Protection Works — the five mechanisms

Protection in Punjab is not a cartoon. It's a set of institutional pressure points. Each mechanism below has at least one documented example. The open question is always: who benefits, and who signed the order?

Mechanism 1 · Police transfers & reinstatements

  • CM Mann: 10,000 police transferred after finding the peddler–police nexus (Feb 2025).
  • IPS Paramraj Singh Umranangal — suspended 2019 as accused in the 2015 Bargari firing + an NDPS case — reinstated Jul 2024 on an HC order. Court-ordered, not political — but the suspension/reinstation cycle shows how officers with drug cases come back.
  • Ex-SSP Raj Jit Hundal — SIT found ₹13 Cr of unexplained transactions and "sudden rise in assets"; HC refused him anticipatory bail (Oct 2023).
  • Retired DGP Shashi Kant (who ran 17-year anti-drug operations) publicly stated parties, sitting and former ministers, MLAs, and senior officers are "hand-in-glove" with smugglers; his 90-name list was handed to the SAD-BJP government and did not produce prosecutions.

Mechanism 2 · Prisons & parole

  • Hoshiarpur Central Jail (Jul 2026): inmate video goes viral → SIT → 12 arrests incl. Dy Superintendent Harbhajan Singh and warden — phones, drugs, and lakh-scale transactions inside a central jail.
  • Ferozepur jail (Jul 2026): fresh probe against senior jail officers — 2005–12 racket allegations, 64 drug deaths, 256-case list claimed by a former official.
  • Shishan Mittal — the drug inspector whose arrest was officially described as a link between jails and street networks.
  • "Harry" — alleged parole-and-flee (Fazilka → Dubai). Not verified. If true, the DC/SSP who signed are the protectors, not the party.
  • Central action: NCB's PITNDPS detentions (2024–25) moving kingpins like Billa Havellian to high-security jails outside Punjab — showing prisons are a known operational risk.

Mechanism 3 · Regulatory appointments & licensing

  • The drug inspector is the person who licenses, inspects, and raids the very trade. Mittal's case — arrested as a "kingpin," then reported reinstated to a headquarters drug-samples post — is the single strongest regulatory-capture candidate on record. Confirm or deny: publish the appointment order.
  • Pharma licensing (FDA, Punjab) is a state power: 72 Lakh pregabalin units seized in 2024; 520 chemist licences suspended and 22 cancelled Apr 2025–Sep 2026. The volume shows how much flows through licensed channels.
  • NCB's Nov 2026 freeze (₹20.10 Cr, 25 properties) shows the diversion pipeline runs from manufacturers in other states — Punjab is the consumption market, not the source.

Mechanism 4 · The sealed envelope

  • Feb 2018: STF chief Harpreet Sidhu files his report on the drug-police-politics nexus in sealed cover at the P&H HC. A copy goes to the ED's Niranjan Singh. The state files its "opinion-cum-status report" — also sealed. May 2018.
  • Dec 2017–May 2018: the HC-constituted SIT (Chattopadhyaya) files four sealed reports on police complicity (Hundal, Inderjit Singh, asset jumps). Three opened in 2023 — five years later. The HC's wording then: action on the reports is up to the state "in case they wish to do so."
  • Dec 2021: the 2018 STF report finally becomes the basis of the Majithia FIR — 3+ years later, under a new CM, 8 weeks before elections.
  • This is the quietest and most effective protection mechanism: the evidence exists, is filed, and waits. It serves every government and punishes nobody.

Mechanism 5 · Political accusation as a substitute for accountability

Since 2014, every government in Punjab has accused its predecessor of protecting drugs — and every opposition has accused the incumbent of the same. The pattern:

  • 2015–17 (SAD-BJP in power): Congress demands court-monitored CBI probe; Congress files HC petitions; AAP joins. No CBI probe ever granted for the political-nexus angle.
  • 2017–22 (Congress in power): STF formed; report sealed; Majithia FIR filed 8 weeks before the 2022 polls; own party's president (Sidhu) accuses the CM's office of suppressing the report.
  • 2022–now (AAP in power): Majithia arrested Jun 2025 (DA case); Jhota killed; BJP's Nasha Mukt Punjab Yatra (Sep 2026); Shah's "drug-free Punjab by 31 Dec 2029" promise; Jakhar's three-part allegation burst (Oct 2026); AAP hits back that drugs now flow from BJP-ruled states.
  • The structural problem: the agency that should break the party nexus (central agencies under a BJP that was the SAD's ally 2007–17) is the same party running the loudest campaign. Cross-party verification does not exist. That is why a court-supervised, party-neutral mechanism (Part 8) is the only durable fix.

(b) The evidence offered — assessed

Strip away the rhetoric and the case against personal Kejriwal involvement rests on four items: an un-released census (a public-health survey with a real budget line), a reported (unconfirmed) reinstatement of an inspector (an administrative decision with a paper trail that simply hasn't been published), prison drug rackets (real, but at warder level in every documented case), and a murder (real, but the gun is identified). None of these four items, even if all true, is evidence that Kejriwal personally profited from or directed the drug economy. The "business strategy from Kejriwal and Sisodia" line is, in the words of the press agency that carried it, Jakhar's claim that "only this duo was capable of hatching up such a sinister plan" — an assertion about motive, not a finding of fact.

(c) The AAP government's response

As of 3 Nov 2026: no formal, document-backed response to Jakhar's three-part allegation has been published (The Statesman: "No fresh response from the AAP government… was available"). AAP's standing position is that the census is a dependency-assessment for rehabilitation, that its anti-drug drive has produced record convictions and seizures, and it has counter-charged that 80% of drug trade now comes via BJP-ruled states and central ports (Kejriwal, 15 Sep 2026). A non-response to a specific, checkable allegation is itself an accountability data point — it leaves the claimable facts (Mittal's appointment order, Harry's parole file, the census report) unpublished.

(d) The verdict, in plain words

On the current public record: there is no credible, independently verifiable evidence that Arvind Kejriwal personally profited from or directed Punjab's drug economy. If the answer is "no on the current record," this dossier says exactly that.

What the record does establish, without editorialising:

  • Institutional failure under the AAP government: documented (jail rackets, the Mittal arrest inside its own cadre, Jhota's death, an unreleased ₹150–250 Cr survey).
  • A transparency failure: documented (census report, Mittal appointment order, Harry's parole file — all claimable, none published).
  • Governmental complicity in the trade: not established. No document links the Mann government or any AAP leader to directing, financing, or profiting from trafficking.
  • Personal involvement by Kejriwal: not established. No evidence exists on the public record. The 2016 Majithia allegation was made and formally retracted. The 2026 "sinister plan" allegation is a press-conference claim.

What would change this verdict: (1) a court order or ED/CBI finding tying AAP leadership to drug money; (2) the census report containing commercial-mapping data that has no public-health purpose; (3) the Mittal appointment order showing political direction; (4) Harry's parole file showing executive-level intervention. None of these has appeared. Until one does, the honest answer stays: documented failure, documented transparency gap, no documented personal involvement.

Why tens of thousands of arrests haven't dismantled the economy

  • The arrest pyramid is upside down. 85,000 peddlers and users arrested (AAP term) vs. a handful of financier-level prosecutions and zero political-level convictions. MHA's own data shows convictions track cases — and cases are registered mostly at the street level. The "30% interception" problem: researchers and the DGP have both said a fixed share of supply is simply never caught; the rest is caught at the bottom where it's easiest and cheapest.
  • Conviction rate measures the bottom, not the top. 88% is an achievement of prosecution discipline (60-point checklists, trained IOs, special courts) — not evidence the big fish are falling. NCB's national conviction rate is ~60%; Punjab's state-level 88% comes from small-case throughput.
  • Asset enforcement is the weak link. ED attached ₹180 Cr in the Bhola case (2017). The Majithia DA case shows the state's own mechanism (Vigilance) is the one chasing politician assets — and it took 11 years from Bhola's arrest to that chargesheet. Financier money keeps circulating while street money is frozen.
  • Substitution, not eradication. Poppy husk restricted → heroin. Heroin pricier/scarcer → pharma (pregabalin, tramadol, alprazolam). The DGP's "80% pharma" figure (2025) — widely cited, sourcing not yet published — and NCB's Nov 2026 ₹20.10 Cr freeze show the market already moved. Enforcement chasing heroin in 2026 is fighting the 2015 war.
  • The border is a losing fight on current tech. 3 drone droppings in 2021, 200+ in 2025, by day. Anti-drone guns and "Baaz" help; the smugglers adapt faster. Central (BSF) responsibility, state pain.
Bottom line: the arrest numbers are real and impressive. They are also structurally incapable of breaking the economy, because they stop where the money and the protection start. Every government in Punjab since 2014 has run this same pyramid. The difference between governments is what they do above the pyramid — and on the record, nobody has done enough, and the mechanisms that would do it (independent probes, published records, asset tracing) have never been built.

9. What Must Happen — the accountability agenda

Specific, checkable, not a manifesto. Six demands, each tied to a document or a court.

  1. Unseal everything. The 2018 STF (Harpreet Sidhu) report and the 2018 SIT (Chattopadhyaya) reports — including the still-sealed personal report of 8 May 2018 — to be unsealed with a national-security review by an independent judicial panel, not the state government. This is the single highest-leverage action: it contains the findings on police and political complicity that everyone argues about and nobody has read in full.
  2. Release the drug census. Report, methodology, and raw data — to an independent public-health body (PGIMER is the obvious home). If the "commercial map" theory is right, the data will show it. If it's wrong, the government gets its reputation. Either way, the ₹150–250 Cr question is answered by a document, not a press conference.
  3. Publish the Mittal file. Bail order + conditions, suspension order, reinstatement order, appointment order, and whether any ED referral was recommended or rejected. One RTI + one gazette notification. The most checkable allegation in this dossier should not remain uncheckable.
  4. Independent probe into prison rackets. Hoshiarpur and Ferozepur (and the 2005–12 Ferozepur allegations, 64 deaths) before a CBI or judicial commission — because the state police investigating the state's own jail rot has a conflict of interest that voters can see.
  5. Parole reform + the Harry file. Publish parole/furlough orders for NDPS and organised-crime convicts 2022–2026; a parole board with published criteria. Produce Harry's parole order and absconding FIR status — or confirm the claim is false.
  6. Make protection expensive and visible. (a) Transfer orders for SHO+ published with rationale. (b) Any drug-cadre official arrested on corruption/NDPS charges suspended pending trial, by rule not discretion. (c) Quarterly public register of assets attached/frozen in drug cases (ED + VB + NCB), with case numbers — so citizens can see if the money trail is being pursued. (d) HC protocol: automatic review of sealed investigative reports after a fixed period.

Drugs are now the defining issue: BJP's Nasha Mukt Yatra + Shah's "31 Dec 2029" deadline; AAP's conviction-rate war; Congress mining Jhota; SAD defending its leader from the DA trial.
